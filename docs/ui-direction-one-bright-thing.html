One typeface, for a game made of numbers

The whole product declares a single family: the system UI stack. No display face, no numeric face, no @font-face. There are 160 tabular-figure treatments and not one of them is set in a typeface anybody chose. The game also renders differently for every player — SF Pro, Segoe, Roboto.

Near-black ground, one bright accent. That is one of the three looks generated design converges on right now. The mechanics are fixed — the ramp is real, contrast passes — but the identity is the one you get by not choosing.

02 — The direction

One Bright Thing

Every dark dashboard spends a saturated accent everywhere: buttons, links, active states, chart series, focus rings. When the accent is everywhere it stops meaning anything, which is the brief's first rule — if everything is important, nothing is important — expressed in colour.

So: hue carries meaning only — good, bad, warning, and nothing else. Light carries importance. Pure white is rationed to exactly one figure per screen: whatever is closest to ending the run this week. Everything else steps down through the ramp we already built.

The planes carry structure. Light carries priority. Hue carries state. Three jobs, three channels, no overlap — and the screen gets a focal point without gaining a single element.

The periwinkle #7C9AFF is retired as a general accent and survives only as a chart series colour, where a series genuinely needs a hue. Per-sector theming stays: it already rebinds one variable, so it costs nothing and it is the one place a decorative hue earns its keep.

Green and red move off the framework defaults — small shifts, but they stop the two most-seen colours in the game from being the ones every Tailwind site ships.

03 — The signature

The runway is the top edge

The defining fact of this game is that the money runs out. Right now that fact is a number in a rail, sharing weight with eight others.

Proposal: the app's top edge is the runway. A two-pixel rule spanning the full width, lit for the proportion of runway remaining against the longest you have ever held. At 153 weeks you never notice it. At 14 weeks it is a short amber stub across a dark screen, and it is the loudest thing in the product — without adding a card, a badge, or a notification.

It costs two pixels of chrome and removes a metric tile. It satisfies never make the player hunt for something urgent and less noise at the same time, which is rare enough to be worth the risk.

05 — Typography

Give the numbers a voice

This is the change with the highest identity-per-byte in the whole proposal, and the only one that costs a download.

RoleNowProposedWhy
FiguresSystem UI, tnumA self-hosted variable grotesque, tight tracking, tabular by defaultThe content of this game is its numbers. They should be the thing with personality, not the thing inheriting it.
ProseSystem UIUnchanged — system UIIt reads well, it costs nothing, and it is the right neutral against a characterful numeric face.
Data / code—System monospaceSeeds, ids, and the replay hash are not prose and should not pretend to be.

Self-hosting is compatible with the CSP as it stands — the font comes from our own origin, so font-src 'self' needs no new host and script-src is untouched. A subset of figures, punctuation and Latin is roughly 25–40 kB, against a 954 kB entry chunk. That is the entire cost.

06 — Honestly

What this risks

Rationing white needs policing

One bright figure per screen is a rule that decays the moment two people disagree about which figure. It needs to be decided by the engine — the metric closest to ending the run — not by whoever writes the next screen.

The runway rule is not free

It is ambient, and ambient signals are ignorable. It should replace a tile, not sit alongside one, or it is decoration. And it needs a non-colour channel for the same reason every other signal here does.

A font is a permanent dependency

Licensing, subsetting, a FOUT to design around, and 25–40 kB on every first load. Worth it once, if the face is right. Not worth doing twice.

This is identity, not architecture

None of it fixes navigation depth, duplicated metrics, or buried decisions. The UX audit running alongside this is what addresses those, and it matters more.
