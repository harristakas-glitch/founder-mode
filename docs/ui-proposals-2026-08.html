Founder Mode · UI direction · August 2026

Three ways out of flat.

The current build has one visual plane, two working type sizes, and sixteen identical rectangles per screen. Below: what is actually wrong, measured — then three complete directions, rendered, so the choice is between real alternatives rather than one option to approve.

“our UI overall is not top class, not even average, it’s boring and old school… we should be more sleek and fresh and clean.” — the brief, verbatim

01

What is actually wrong

Not “needs more whitespace”. Six measurements, each checkable by opening the file named under it. Surfaces are compared in CIE L* (perceptual lightness, 0–100), because WCAG’s contrast ratio is meaningless this far down the dark end — its +0.05 flare term makes every near-black pair score ~1.1:1. Rule used: ΔL* ≥ 4 is a plane step a player can see; ΔL* ≤ 2 is a rounding error.

And the shadow cannot rescue it: --elev-2 is 0 2px 8px -2px rgba(0,0,0,.5) — pure black on a page already at L* 4.02. Its darkest possible pixel lands at L* 1.90, ΔL* −2.12. What it buys

  • Density with air. Same information as the control in less vertical space, because 16 sets of padding + border + radius are gone.
  • A real first look. One 44px figure and one gold “Needs you” tag are the only two things competing; everything else is deliberately quiet.
  • Scales to Fundraising. A table does not get worse at 40 rows; a grid of cards does.
  • Cheapest to build. Delete CARD, rewrite Panel as a heading + rule, keep every screen’s structure.

What it costs / risks

  • Unforgiving. With no boxes to hide behind, every alignment error and every long label is visible. The type has to be right.
  • Reads austere if the accent is used more than twice per screen. This direction lives or dies on restraint.
  • Least “fun”. Furthest from a game, closest to a terminal. Good for Career, arguable for Arena.
  • Emoji have to go — they are the loudest thing on a page this quiet.

What it buys

  • Depth that survives a screenshot. Four planes at ΔL* 5–6 read on a phone in daylight; today’s ramp does not read on a good monitor indoors.
  • One hero, decided by the engine. The 56px figure is whatever is closest to killing the run this week — the sim already computes it in attentionItems().
  • Least disruptive. Screen structure is untouched; this is a rewrite of @theme, CARD, Panel and StatCard.
  • Most “game”. Closest to what a modern management sim looks like; best fit for Arena.

What it costs / risks

  • Four planes need policing. Without a rule for which plane a thing lives on, it degenerates back into soup within a month.
  • Still card-shaped. Fundraising’s 15 panels stay 15 panels — this direction makes them legible, not fewer.
  • The gradient in index.css:113 has to go. It is the cause of the inversion and cannot be tuned around.
  • Higher pixel cost per number than Ledger: ~15% more vertical space for the same data.

What it buys

  • It answers “what do I do this week?” — the question the Dashboard currently makes the player derive from 16 boxes.
  • The prose finally gets a reading size. 92 blocks of genuinely good writing stop being fine print.
  • Decisions get priced on the screen where they are taken — the exact gap named in docs/onboarding-design.md §1C, closed by layout rather than by a new teaching system.
  • Best on a phone. A folded list is native to the bottom-tab shell that just shipped; the other two are desktop-first designs adapted down.

What it costs / risks

  • Hiding numbers in a simulation is dangerous. An expert wants all forty at once. Mitigated by the pin and the Brief ⇄ Everything toggle — but that is two states to design and test, not one.
  • Most expensive. Needs a new <Section> component, a reorder of Dashboard.tsx, and per-section summary strings the engine does not currently produce.
  • One more click between the player and a number they used to see. That is a real cost, not a rounding error.
  • Only works if every summary line is genuinely informative. A folded section whose summary says “5 metrics” is worse than the box it replaced.

The three are not variations. They disagree about what should carry hierarchy, and that disagreement has consequences all the way down to how many components get rewritten.

What is true whichever wins

RuleWhy
Adjacent planes ≥ ΔL* 4, measured after alpha compositingToday’s ramp is ΔL* 2–4 and inverts under the body gradient. If a surface is translucent over a gradient, the ordering must hold at both ends of that gradient — or the gradient goes.
No pure-black shadows as the primary depth cue--elev-2 is ΔL* −2.12 against the page. On dark, the plane step does ~80% of the work and a top inner highlight beats a drop shadow.
One scale, ≤ 8 steps, no fractional pixelsEvery text-[12.5px] gets deleted, not migrated. This is the single highest-leverage change on the list.
A normal weight exists, and prose defaults to it2 of 232 weight declarations are normal. Bold becomes a signal again only once it is rare.
Ink is the default text colour; grey is the exceptionThe current 460 : 113 ratio inverts.
The mobile shell is untouchedBottom tab bar, More sheet, contextual action bar, pad-bottom-safe, dvh, 16px inputs, haptics. All three directions were designed on top of it.
Focus ring, reduced-motion, never-colour-alone survive verbatimThese are already right. index.css:146, :559, TrendBadge’s ▲/▼.
Per-sector accent rebinding survivestheme.ts retints the whole UI per market. All three keep it; they differ only in how much accent gets used.

Recommendation, since one was asked for. B · Cockpit for the next release: it is the smallest change that removes the actual defect (one plane, no elevation, no scale), it keeps every screen’s structure so nothing regresses, and it is the most “fresh and sleek” of the three at the lowest risk. Then take C · Briefing’s decision-pricing pattern — the priced trade-off block — and apply it to the Inbox only, which is where the onboarding audit already said the damage is. A · Ledger is the right long-term answer for Career specifically, once the type discipline exists to sustain it.
